## 1. Glycemic Targets & Monitoring

### A1c Target Ranges (by Patient Characteristics)

**Table B-1: HbA1c Target Ranges by Comorbidity & Life Expectancy**

| **Physiologic Age / Comorbidity** | **No/Mild Microvascular** | **Moderate Microvascular** | **Advanced Microvascular** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Absent major comorbidity**<br/>(>10–15 years life expectancy) | 6.0–7.0% | 7.0–8.0% | 7.5–8.5% |
| **Present major comorbidity**<br/>(5–10 years life expectancy) | 7.0–8.0% | 7.5–8.5% | 7.5–8.5% |
| **Marked/End-stage comorbidity**<br/>(<5 years life expectancy) | 8.0–9.0% | 8.0–9.0% | 8.0–9.0% |

**Key definitions:**
- **Mild microvascular**: Early background retinopathy, moderately increased albuminuria, mild neuropathy
- **Moderate microvascular**: Pre-proliferative retinopathy, severely increased albuminuria, demonstrable peripheral neuropathy
- **Advanced microvascular**: Proliferative retinopathy, serum creatinine >2.0 mg/dL, insensate extremities, autonomic neuropathy
- **Major comorbidity**: Significant CVD, severe CKD, severe COPD, severe chronic liver disease, recent cerebrovascular disease, life-threatening malignancy

### General A1c Recommendations

- **Primary recommendation (Recommendation #10)**: Target HbA1c range of **7.0–8.5% for most patients**, if it can be safely achieved (Weak recommendation)
- **Individualization (Recommendation #9)**: Set individualized HbA1c target based on clinician's appraisal of risk-benefit ratio, patient characteristics, complications, comorbidities, and life expectancy (Weak recommendation)

### Important Monitoring Principles

- **Avoid medication changes** based on single HbA1c test slightly outside target range, especially if discordant with self-monitoring of blood glucose (SMBG)
- **African Americans caveat**: Average HbA1c 0.4% higher than Whites; use caution changing medications without considering SMBG
- **Coefficient of variation** should be considered (lab-specific); example: HbA1c 8.0% with CV of 3% = measured 7.8–8.2% range (1 SD)
- **Avoid estimated average glucose (eAG)** derived from HbA1c

### High Glycemic Variability (Recommendation #8)

- Use high glycemic variability over time (HbA1c or fasting glucose fluctuation) as **prognostic indicator for risk of hypoglycemia, morbidity, and mortality** (Weak recommendation)

### Continuous Glucose Monitoring (Recommendation #11)

- **Real-time CGM** (rtCGM) in insulin-treated adults NOT at glycemic goals: decreases hypoglycemia and improves HbA1c (Weak recommendation)
- **Not for**: Intermittently scanned CGM (isCGM) or flash CGM without alarms (insufficient evidence)
- Context: Recommendation applies to insulin-treated; evidence unclear for non-insulin patients or those already at goal

## 2. Pharmacotherapy

### Metformin (Biguanide) – First-Line Agent

**Mechanism**: A1c reduction 1.0–1.5% (monotherapy)  
**Hypoglycemia risk**: No  
**CV effects**: Neutral (potential ASCVD benefit)  

**Contraindications & Precautions**:
- **Contraindicated**: eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m² (do not initiate if eGFR <45)
- May continue at reduced dose with eGFR <45 with monitoring
- Increased lactic acidosis risk with acute HF, dehydration, excessive alcohol, renal impairment, sepsis

**Adverse effects**: GI disturbance (diarrhea, nausea), B12 deficiency (rarely anemia)  
**Dosing**: Slow titration, take with food, use sustained-action formulation

**Special handling**:
- Hold temporarily for radiologic studies with contrast and procedures

### SGLT-2 Inhibitors

**Mechanism**: A1c reduction 0.5–1.0% (monotherapy)  
**Hypoglycemia risk**: No  
**CV & renal benefits**: 
- ASCVD benefit: empagliflozin, canagliflozin
- Renal benefit (moderate): empagliflozin, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin
- HF benefit: Proven reduction in HF hospitalizations

**Contraindications**:
- eGFR <20–30 (check specific labeling)
- Pregnancy/breastfeeding
- High risk of DKA (including euglycemic DKA)

**Adverse effects**:
- Genitourinary infections (increased risk)
- DKA
- Volume depletion/hypotension
- Bone fractures (canagliflozin)
- Lower limb amputations (increased with canagliflozin in CANVAS trial)
- Initial reversible increase in serum creatinine; long-term improvement

**Dosing & administration**:
- Oral, without regard to food
- Hold ≥3 days before surgery
- Cardiorenal benefits realized at initial doses
- Glucose-lowering efficacy reduced at lower eGFR, but cardiorenal benefits retained

### GLP-1 Receptor Agonists

**Mechanism**: A1c reduction 1.0–2.0% (monotherapy)  
**Hypoglycemia risk**: No  
**CV & renal benefits**:
- ASCVD benefit: liraglutide, dulaglutide, semaglutide (injectable)
- Renal benefit (primarily reduced albuminuria): liraglutide, dulaglutide, semaglutide (injectable)
- Weight loss: Moderate to very high (efficacy depends on agent and dose)

**Contraindications**:
- Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma
- Multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome 2
- CrCl <15 mL/min (lixisenatide <30; exenatide)
- Proliferative diabetic retinopathy (semaglutide) — risk must be balanced against progressive retinopathy from poor control
- Current gallbladder disease
- Pregnancy

**Adverse effects**:
- GI: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation
- Gastroparesis (at high risk)
- Injection site reactions
- At high risk for pancreatitis
- Increased risk of diabetic retinopathy complications (semaglutide vs. placebo in SUSTAIN-6)
- Possible renal impairment if severe dehydration from GI effects

**Dosing & administration**:
- All injected subcutaneously except oral semaglutide
- Frequency: 1–2 times daily or weekly (depends on agent)
- Avoid concurrent use with DPP-4 inhibitor or GIP/GLP-1 agonist

### GIP/GLP-1 Agonist (Tirzepatide)

**Mechanism**: A1c reduction 2.0–2.5% (monotherapy) — highest A1c reduction  
**Hypoglycemia risk**: No  
**CV/renal effects**: Neutral based on available evidence (prospective studies ongoing for ASCVD, HF)  
**Weight loss**: Very high

**Contraindications**:
- Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma
- Multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome
- Gastroparesis, at high risk of pancreatitis
- Current gallbladder disease
- Pregnancy

**Adverse effects**:
- GI: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation
- Injection site reactions
- Possible renal impairment if dehydration

**Dosing & administration**:
- Subcutaneous injection once weekly
- Supplied as single-dose pens
- **OCP interaction**: May decrease efficacy (especially 4 weeks post-initiation and dose increases); alternative contraception recommended

### Cardiovascular/Renal Indications (Recommendations #19–24)

**Recommendation #19** (Strong): Adults with T2DM AND ASCVD → **GLP-1 RA or SGLT-2i with proven CV benefits** (to decrease MACE)

**Recommendation #20** (Weak): Adults with T2DM at high risk ASCVD (CKD, LVH, HF) → **GLP-1 RA or SGLT-2i with proven CV benefits**

**Recommendation #21** (Strong): Adults with T2DM AND HF → **SGLT-2 inhibitor** (prevent HF hospitalizations)

**Recommendation #22** (Strong): Adults with T2DM AND CKD → **SGLT-2 inhibitors with proven renal protection**

**Recommendation #23** (Strong): Adults with T2DM AND CKD, NOT good candidates for SGLT-2i → **GLP-1 RA with proven renal protection** (improve macroalbuminuria)

**Recommendation #24** (Weak): Consider adding SGLT-2i or GLP-1 RA **even if at A1c goal** if patient has CVD or renal disease

### Older Adults & Hypoglycemia Risk (Recommendation #25)

**Recommendation #25** (Weak): Adults ≥65 years → Prioritize drug classes OTHER than insulin, sulfonylureas, meglitinides to minimize hypoglycemia risk

### DPP-4 Inhibitors

**A1c reduction**: 0.5–1.0%  
**Hypoglycemia**: No  
**CV/renal**: Neutral for ASCVD; potential HF risk (saxagliptin)  
**Contraindication**: At high risk of pancreatitis; pregnancy  
**Note**: Avoid concurrent use with GLP-1 and GIP/GLP-1 agonists

### Sulfonylureas & Meglitinides

**A1c reduction**: 1.0–1.5% (SU), 0.5–1.0% (meglitinide)  
**Hypoglycemia risk**: Yes (SU > meglitinide)  
**Special concerns**: Increased risk in elderly, renal/hepatic impairment, poor intake, certain antimicrobials (fluoroquinolones, SMX-TMP)  
**Note**: Deprioritized in older adults due to hypoglycemia risk

## 4. Kidney Disease & ACE-I/ARB Management

### Nephropathy Screening & Monitoring (Sidebar 3)

**Monitoring cadence**:
- **Monitor urine microalbumin/creatinine ratio at least annually** (screening and surveillance)

**ACE-I / ARB Use**:
- **Consider ACE inhibitor or ARB** in patients with:
  - Hypertension (HTN)
  - **Moderately increased albuminuria** (microalbuminuria)
  - Chronic kidney disease (CKD)

**Additional recommendations**:
- Use **SGLT-2 inhibitor or GLP-1 RA** in patients with diabetic nephropathy
- **Avoid nephrotoxic medications** (NSAIDs)
- Reference VA/DoD CPG for CKD for guideline-directed treatments and targets

### eGFR Thresholds (Summary)

- **eGFR <45**: Do NOT initiate metformin; may continue at reduced dose with monitoring
- **eGFR <30**: Metformin contraindicated
- **eGFR <20–30**: SGLT-2 inhibitors (check labeling); glucose-lowering efficacy reduced but cardiorenal benefits retained
- **eGFR <15**: GLP-1 RA concern (lixisenatide <30; exenatide)

## 5. Retinopathy & Eye Screening

### Screening Cadence (Sidebar 2)

**Initial screening**:
- **Dilated fundus examination by eye care professional OR retinal imaging** with interpretation by qualified reader
- **Perform within 6 months of new T2DM diagnosis**

**Surveillance screening**:
- **Biennial screening for retinopathy** for patients with NO history of retinopathy on all prior examinations

**More frequent screening**:
- Indicated for patients with:
  - Additional risk factors
  - Existing retinopathy
  - Risk factors for progression of retinopathy

### Protocol-Only Insight

**This represents a "protocol step HEDIS measures miss"**: HEDIS DM-03 typically measures "retinal exam performed" (yes/no in measurement year), but does NOT enforce the **within-6-months-of-diagnosis** urgency or differentiate **biennial intervals for disease-free** vs. more frequent for those with existing or progressing retinopathy. This is richer clinical guidance.

## 6. Pregnancy & Contraindications

### Drug Classes Contraindicated in Pregnancy

**Absolute contraindications**:
- **SGLT-2 inhibitors** — pregnancy/breastfeeding
- **GLP-1 receptor agonists** — pregnancy
- **GIP/GLP-1 agonists** — pregnancy
- **DPP-4 inhibitors** — pregnancy
- **TZDs** (thiazolidinediones) — pregnancy
- **Metformin** — traditionally cautioned, but current guidance: use caution; consult CKD/pregnancy co-management

**Preferred in pregnancy**:
- **Insulin** (various formulations available)

**Note**: Refer to VA/DoD CPG for Pregnancy for comprehensive peripartum diabetes management.

## 7. Escalation & Deprescribing Rules

### Intensification vs. De-intensification (Recommendation #8 context)

**When to intensify**:
- HbA1c above target range (individualized)
- After 3 months of stable therapy at target dose (not immediate)

**When to de-intensify / deprescribe**:
- HbA1c consistently below target with recurrent hypoglycemia
- Changed clinical circumstances (renal decline, HF onset)
- Single HbA1c slightly outside range should NOT trigger medication change alone

### No Explicit "X-Month Rule" for Most Medications

**Exception**: Real-time CGM recommended for insulin-treated patients "not achieving glycemic goals" — implies trial duration before escalation decision, but exact duration not specified in this CPG.

## 8. Protocol Steps That HEDIS Measures Miss

### Key Gaps Between HEDIS & Protocol

**HEDIS DM-01 (Glycemic Control)**
- Measures: HbA1c ≤7% or >9% (binary outcomes)
- **Misses**: Individualized A1c targets by comorbidity/life expectancy (Table B-1); de-intensification for overtreatment

**HEDIS DM-03 (Retinal Exam)**
- Measures: Exam performed in year
- **Misses**:
  - **6-month urgency for new diagnosis** (protocol step)
  - **Biennial cadence for disease-free** vs. annual/as-needed for existing disease
  - Risk-stratified screening based on prior retinopathy

**HEDIS DM-02 (Nephropathy/Albuminuria Screening)**
- Measures: Test performed in year
- **Misses**:
  - **Annual monitoring cadence** (protocol is explicit)
  - **Differentiated ACE/ARB initiation** based on microalbuminuria presence + HTN/CKD status (not just presence of albuminuria)
  - SGLT-2i/GLP-1 RA **in addition to** ACE/ARB for renal protection

**HEDIS DM-04 (BP Control) & General**
- Measures: Isolated BP target achievement
- **Misses**:
  - **Cardio-renal indication-driven SGLT-2i/GLP-1 RA** (even if A1c at goal) — Recommendation #24
  - Synchronized monitoring of eGFR, albuminuria, BP, and drug choice (SGLT-2i as cardio-renal guardian)
  - **Hypoglycemia risk stratification** by age, kidney function, drug class — Recommendation #25

**HEDIS DM-05 (Statin Use)**
- Measures: Statin use (not in this document extract, but by implication)
- **Misses**: Outcome-based SGLT-2i/GLP-1 RA CV benefit prioritization over metformin monotherapy in some populations

## 9. Lifestyle & Diabetes Self-Management Education (DSMES)

### DSMES Recommendation (#7 — Strong)

- **Strongly recommend diabetes self-management education and support** (not Weak; Strong recommendation)

## 10. Nutrition Interventions (for Protocol Context)

### Mediterranean Diet (Recommendation #12 — Weak for)

- Improves glycemic control, body weight, hypertension
- High vegetables, legumes, fruits, nuts, seeds, unrefined grains, olive oil
- Moderate fish/poultry, low/mod wine, low red meat

### Carbohydrate Distribution (Recommendation #13 — Weak for)

- Target **13–50% of daily caloric intake from carbohydrates** (individualized per RDN)

### Vegetarian Pattern (Recommendation #14 — Weak for)

- Improves glycemic control and weight loss
- Requires RDN support for nutritional adequacy (protein, B12, iron)

### Intermittent Fasting (Recommendation #15 — Weak against)

- Not recommended; insufficient or equivalent evidence vs. standard dietary approaches
